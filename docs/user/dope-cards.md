# DOPE cards

Go to **Ammo → (a load) → DOPE Card**.

A printable drop table for one rifle, one load and one set of conditions — the thing
that goes in the stock pouch, works with flat batteries, and does not need to be
unlocked with cold fingers.

## Generating one

1. Select the rifle and the load
2. Set the conditions you expect to shoot in
3. Generate

You get a table from **100 to 1000 yards in 100-yard steps**, with elevation in your
chosen angular unit and wind holds across a range of wind speeds.

## Exporting

**Print** produces a **PDF**, which you can send to a printer, share, or keep on the
phone.

Printing it is the point. A card on the phone is a second thing that can run out of
battery; a card in the pouch is not.

## Cards are conditions-specific

The table is computed for the conditions you entered. Take a card generated for a
70°F day at 500 feet to a 25°F morning at 7,000 feet and the elevation figures will
be wrong — density altitude is most of what determines drop, and it will have moved
a long way.

Practical approaches, in rough order of how well they work:

- **Generate a card per expected condition set** — a summer card and a winter card
  for the ranges you actually shoot
- **Generate on the day**, before you leave signal
- **Note the conditions the card was made for** on the card itself, so a stale one
  announces itself rather than quietly misleading you

## Cards versus logged DOPE

A card is a _prediction_. Your [logged DOPE](./logging-dope.md) is a _measurement_.

Where they disagree past a few hundred yards, the log is usually right — it contains
your barrel's real velocity, your scope's real tracking and your load's real
behaviour, none of which the card knows about. Use the card to get on paper at a new
distance; use the log to confirm what you dial.
